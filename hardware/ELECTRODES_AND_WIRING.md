# Build wet first, wire BrainTech-16 for active

The first BrainTech-16 build should use **passive wet electrodes of a single metal at every site, SRB2 and BIAS included**. Sintered Ag/AgCl is the target; gold cups are the budget option if every electrode is gold and each uses chloride paste after abrasion. The leads should be fine stranded wire, 20–40 cm long, sewn into the cap and gathered into one ribbon to the occipital header. **Unshielded leads are adequate at wet-electrode impedances.** Dry through-hair electrodes only work well with a buffer at the electrode, about 2–3 N of spring or strap preload, and preferably driven shields. The passive dry comb on the Cyton gave about 300 µV of 50 Hz hum and lost contact 24 % of the time, which is the expected result without those three things. Two values carried over from the Cyton design need to change on the PCB. **A 1 nF X7R capacitor from each input to AGND turns common-mode mains into differential signal with only about 12–17 dB of attenuation once contacts reach 0.5–2 MΩ.** **A 2.2 kΩ resistor on BIAS lets the bias amplifier push up to its 1.1 mA short-circuit current into the scalp; the target is about 50 µA.** Rev A should move to a 2x13 IDC header so the harness has spare pins for ±2.5 V electrode power, a driven shield and an impedance-excitation line. It should also use small matched C0G input capacitors, put at least 100 kΩ in series with BIAS, and carry unpopulated footprints for a shield driver and a reference buffer. These changes cost cents and keep the board ready for active electrodes without a re-spin. Your own Cyton measurements point the same way: the board's noise floor is 0.10 µV, while the literature puts electrode-skin noise at 1–20 µVrms. **Spend on electrodes and skin prep first.**

One more piece of context matters in India. Kappenman and Luck found that high-impedance sites add low-frequency skin-potential noise, **especially in warm, humid rooms** (mean 27.7 °C), and that this effect mostly disappears at <5 kΩ ([Kappenman & Luck 2010](https://erpinfo.org/s/Kappenman-2010-Psychophys-Impedance-p398.pdf)). On a DC-coupled board, slow drift is more than a filtering nuisance: it can push the input to the rails, and software cannot recover a saturated sample. Indian summers therefore make low, matched impedances more important than the European lab literature implies.

## Contact impedance through hair decides which electrode design works

The literature agrees on one point: once the input impedance is about 1 GΩ, signal amplitude is no longer limited by the amplifier. What limits quality is contact impedance, how mismatched it is across channels, and how stable it stays. With 200 MΩ amplifiers, Ferree found **no significant amplitude change at any EEG frequency from <5 kΩ to 40 kΩ**, but **60 Hz noise rose linearly with impedance and with mismatch** ([Ferree et al. 2001](https://www.eeginfo.com/research/ElectrodeImpedance.pdf)). Laszlo et al. found that passive electrodes, like this board's, gave the cleanest data of any condition "at very low impedance, but not at any impedance >2 kΩ" ([Laszlo et al. 2014](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/a-direct-comparison-of-active-and-passive-amplification-electrode/)). For a passive build, aim for **<10 kΩ on every channel, <5 kΩ on SRB2 and BIAS, and similar values across channels**.

Across matched-subject studies, **dry costs motor-imagery classification about 0–6 percentage points**. A 3-class lower-limb CNN scored 58.44 % dry vs 58.66 % wet with no significant difference ([Sensors 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8513081/)). An 11-channel sLDA scored 56.4 % vs 62.3 % ([Zhang et al. 2025 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12389868/)). The bigger losses come from discarded trials and from one bad contact at C3 or C4, the two sites that carry the left/right difference. That is why dry electrodes belong on an experiment track, not in the first build.

### Active versus passive: buffering at the electrode is what makes dry work

The review by Chi, Jung and Cauwenberghs states it directly: successful dry designs "use either an active electrode circuit to buffer the signal before driving any cabling or alternatively penetrate the skin" ([Chi et al. 2010](https://isn.ucsd.edu/pub/papers/rbme10.pdf)). The evidence on this board's chip is good. An active dry system using **OPA2376 buffers into an ADS1299 at gain 24** drove both a guard ring and the cable shield, and correlated >0.8 with a Nihon Kohden wet system ([arXiv 2503.23338](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.23338)). A low-cost OPA2376 active dry electrode measured **1.3 µVrms of power-line noise vs 2.0 µVrms for wet gold cups** ([Pourahmad & Mahnam 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5156995)). Active electrodes do not fix everything. In a robot-phantom study, active BioSemi electrodes still lost **10 % of SNR to cable sway** ([Symeonidou et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5948545/)). Commercial dry systems (g.SAHARA, DSI-24, CGX Quick-20r) all use active electrodes and/or active shielding ([DSI-24](https://wearablesensing.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Wearable-Sensing-DSI-24-Specification-Sheet_s.pdf); [Quick-20r v2](https://brainvision.com/product/quick-20r-v2/)).

Use a unity-gain OPA2376 per electrode: one half buffers the electrode, the other half drives the local guard and cable shield. Run it from the board's **±2.5 V analog rails** so its output cannot exceed the ADS1299 input range. Buffer the SRB2 reference electrode too; otherwise the impedance imbalance just moves to the reference side. Leave BIAS passive. Adding gain at the electrode costs CMRR through tolerance mismatch and needs more wires ([Pourahmad & Mahnam 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5156995)). Active electrodes also stop ADS1299 lead-off from working: the injected nA current flows into the buffer's ohm-level output, so the board reads the buffer, not the skin. That is why commercial systems measure impedance inside the electrode ([actiCAP](https://www.brainproducts.com/solutions/acticap/)) or over a separate wire ([Guermandi et al. 2015](https://cris.unibo.it/handle/11585/517819)), and why the board needs an IMP_EXC pin.

## Short sewn-in ribbon leads beat shielded cable for wet electrodes

Mains interference reaches the inputs three ways ([Huhta & Webster 1973](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60-HZ-interference-in-electrocardiography.-Huhta-Webster/c78026acd5f66b7cbffc018f88326264ff0cfbe0)). First, displacement current into the body becomes differential signal wherever impedances are mismatched. Second, displacement current flows straight into each lead. Third, magnetic fields induce voltage in the loop the leads form. BioSemi calls unshielded electrode wires "usually the major source" of 50 Hz interference ([BioSemi](https://www.biosemi.com/publications/artikel1.htm)). Shielding has a cost, though. **A shield tied to AGND adds 50–100+ pF per lead.** That lowers the input's common-mode impedance and so increases common-mode-to-differential conversion. TI's ADS129x note shows how mismatch in electrodes, cable RC and protection parts can pull CMR below 60 dB. In the same note, a closed BIAS loop raised measured CMR from 70.6–83.3 dB to **93.3–96.0 dB** ([TI SBAA188](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/sbaa188/sbaa188.pdf)). The same note says portable battery instruments have inherently very high CMR, because almost no current path runs from the board to earth.

The *derived* numbers show why shielding is optional for wet electrodes and needed for dry ones. A lead conductor carries about **72 nA per pF** of capacitance to a 230 V, 50 Hz conductor, and every nA through 1 kΩ of electrode impedance is 1 µV. So 0.1 pF to a nearby mains cord gives about 70 µV at 10 kΩ, but that is a shared-path effect that BIAS and a notch filter largely remove. At 1 MΩ the same coupling gives about 7 mV. Magnetic pickup is about **31 nV per cm² of loop area per µT**. A lead bundle that keeps its loop area to a few cm² makes this negligible; the typical indoor field strength is not sourced. A grounded 100 pF shield brings the 50 Hz common-mode input impedance down to about 32 MΩ. At 10 mV of body common mode, that is about **0.3 µV per kΩ of electrode mismatch**: harmless with gel, damaging with dry contacts. If shields are used, drive them from a buffered common-mode voltage, which is the "guarding" Metting van Rijn named as a key interference fix ([Metting van Rijn et al. 1990](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/BF02441961)). Do not tie them to AGND.

Motion artifacts make the routing argument stronger. In walking tests, heel-strike artifact was **24.70 µV with a cabled cap vs 7.23 µV with a glued, cable-free cap**. During jogging it was **46.03 vs 7.64 µV**. The cable-free design used **0.1 mm enamelled copper sewn into the fabric in a sinusoidal pattern** so it could stretch ([Sensors 2024, PMC11479364](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11479364/)). OpenBCI's own advice is to tape all leads together, because dangling electrodes "severely" affect signals ([OpenBCI docs](https://docs.openbci.com/Troubleshooting/minimizingNoise/)). Graphite-coated low-noise coax solves triboelectric charge in free-hanging cable ([US 4678865](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4678865)). A sewn-in lead that does not sway does not need it.

## Handoff to the PCB designer

The designer's actual input values have not been confirmed, so item 2 is written as "check your values against this table". The baseline below is the OpenBCI Cyton, which uses a **TPD4E1B06 TVS, then 2.2 kΩ, then 1 nF X7R to AGND** per input ([Cyton schematic](https://github.com/OpenBCI/V3_Hardware_Design_Files/blob/master/OpenBCI%20Cyton%20Designs/OBCI_V3_32bit-Schematic.jpg); [Cyton BOM](https://github.com/OpenBCI/V3_Hardware_Design_Files/tree/master/OpenBCI%20Cyton%20Designs/OBCI_Cyton_Plots)). The TI EVM uses 4.99 kΩ + 4.7 nF ([SLAU443B](https://www.ti.com/lit/ug/slau443b/slau443b.pdf)).

1. **Connector: replace the 2x10 with a 2x13 (26-pin) keyed, shrouded male IDC header.** Use the pinout in the table below, mirror it on the silkscreen, and mark pin 1. The harness side uses a female IDC socket with strain relief. *Rationale:* active electrodes need +2.5 V, −2.5 V, a driven shield and an impedance-excitation line, and the 2x10 has no spare pins. *Fallback:* keep the 2x10 and add a keyed 1x5 header (+2.5V_AE, −2.5V_AE, AGND, SHIELD_DRV, IMP_EXC). **Priority: must for rev A.**

| Pin = ribbon conductor | Net | Pin = ribbon conductor | Net |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | SHIELD_DRV (ribbon edge) | 14 | IN9 (ADS #2 CH1) |
| 2 | IN1 (ADS #1 CH1) | 15 | IN10 |
| 3 | IN2 | 16 | IN11 |
| 4 | IN3 | 17 | IN12 |
| 5 | IN4 | 18 | IN13 |
| 6 | IN5 | 19 | IN14 |
| 7 | IN6 | 20 | IN15 |
| 8 | IN7 | 21 | IN16 |
| 9 | IN8 | 22 | AGND (separates IMP_EXC from inputs) |
| 10 | SRB (SRB2 reference) | 23 | IMP_EXC |
| 11 | AGND | 24 | +2.5V_AE |
| 12 | BIAS | 25 | −2.5V_AE |
| 13 | AGND | 26 | SPARE: 0 Ω/DNP to AGND or SHIELD_DRV |

   This order keeps BIAS between two AGND conductors and next to SRB, keeps each ADS1299's eight inputs together, and keeps the AC excitation and supply rails at the far edge. In standard IDC numbering, ribbon conductor *n* lands on pin *n*.

2. **Input shunt capacitors: check your values against this table.** Then fit an **0603 footprint, C0G/NP0, ±1–2 %, default 47 pF** (100 pF if ESP32-S3 RF pickup shows up), with DNP allowed for dry passive experiments and 1 nF C0G for an active-electrode build variant. Use the identical network on the SRB line. *Rationale:* shunt capacitance to AGND converts common mode to differential in proportion to electrode-impedance mismatch. X7R is also a poor match: the Cyton part's "M" suffix in TDK's code denotes ±20 % (verify). **Priority: must for rev A.**

| Shunt C per input (*derived*, 50 Hz, resistive electrodes) | 5k/10k (gel) | 200k/400k | 500k/1M | 1M/2M (dry) | RC corner with 2.2 kΩ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.7 nF (TI EVM, with 4.99 kΩ) | −43 dB | −12 dB | −9.5 dB | −11.5 dB | 6.8 kHz |
| 1 nF (Cyton) | −56 dB | −24 dB | −17 dB | −12 dB | 72 kHz |
| 100 pF | −76 dB | −44 dB | −36 dB | −30 dB | 723 kHz |
| **47 pF (recommended)** | −83 dB | −51 dB | −43 dB | −37 dB | 1.5 MHz |
| 22 pF | −89 dB | −57 dB | −49 dB | −43 dB | 3.3 MHz |

   Every value works for gel. Only ≤100 pF keeps dry contacts usable, and the ADS1299's own 20 pF input capacitance ([TI](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ads1299.pdf)) sets a floor that only buffering removes. Large shunt capacitance is not needed for anti-aliasing, because the delta-sigma converter's on-chip decimation "drastically reduces" that requirement ([TI](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ads1299.pdf)).

3. **Differential-mode capacitor option: add an 0603 footprint from each INxP to the SRB net, DNP by default.** *Rationale:* it filters differential signal without converting common mode, and a TI E2E answer reportedly recommends it (cited only via a search excerpt) ([TI E2E 637339](https://e2e.ti.com/support/data-converters-group/data-converters/f/data-converters-forum/637339/ads1299-schematic-question)). **Priority: nice to have.**

4. **Series resistors: keep 2.2–4.99 kΩ thin-film, 0.1 %, the same value on all 17 lines (16 IN + SRB).** *Rationale:* they limit clamp current into the ADS1299's rail diodes (10 mA abs max) and add only 6–9 nV/√Hz (*derived*). *Decision to log:* they protect the chip, not the patient. Under a single fault such as a damaged input latched to a rail, about 1 mA could flow (*derived*). Raising them to 47–100 kΩ would add 28–41 nV/√Hz, which is still small next to the 1–20 µVrms electrode-skin noise, and the footprint stays the same. **Priority: must (existing); value review is nice to have.**

5. **TVS: keep a TPD4E1B06-class array (≤0.5 nA leakage, 0.7 pF) at the connector, before the series R, with layout that allows DNP.** *Rationale:* *derived*, 0.5 nA × 1 MΩ = 0.5 mV of offset and 12.7 fA/√Hz of shot noise, both negligible even for dry contacts. Avoid generic TVS parts with µA leakage or tens of pF ([TI TPD4E1B06](https://www.ti.com/product/TPD4E1B06)). **Priority: must for rev A (fit it; DNP is the experiment option).**

6. **BIAS: make the total series resistance ≥100 kΩ.** Use two footprints, RP1 outside the loop and RP2 inside the feedback (TI's split arrangement). Keep the feedback RC footprints so the loop can be re-tuned: datasheet 1 MΩ ‖ 1.5 nF, EVM 390 kΩ / 10 nF. *Rationale:* TI recommends **≤50 µA single-fault body-drive current, i.e. ≥100 kΩ at a 5 V supply span** ([TI SBAA188](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/sbaa188/sbaa188.pdf)). The BIAS amplifier can source 1.1 mA short-circuit ([TI ADS1299](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ads1299.pdf)), and the Cyton's 2.2 kΩ does not limit that. Use only chip 1's BIAS amplifier. **Priority: must for rev A.**

7. **Shield-driver footprint: dual RRIO op-amp (OPA2376, SOIC-8 or SOT-23-8) on AVDD/AVSS, DNP.** Select its input with 0 Ω jumpers from (a) the BIAS common-mode/BIASINV node through a high-value resistor, (b) the buffered SRB, or (c) AGND. Its output goes through 100 Ω–1 kΩ, with an RC snubber footprint, to SHIELD_DRV. *Rationale:* do not hang nF-scale harness capacitance on BIASOUT, which is specified into only 50 kΩ ‖ 10 pF. TI's own EVM ships an unpopulated shield-drive footprint (BIAS_SHD/JP17) ([SLAU443B](https://www.ti.com/lit/ug/slau443b/slau443b.pdf)). **Priority: must (footprint) for rev A; populate later.**

8. **SRB reference buffer: use the second half of the shield-driver op-amp as an optional SRB2 buffer with a 0 Ω bypass**, as on the EVM. *Rationale:* 16 inputs × 200 pA puts about 3.2 nA DC into the earlobe electrode. That is negligible with gel (32 µV at 10 kΩ) but 3.2 mV at 1 MΩ (*derived*). The EVM notes buffering adds some noise ([SLAU443B](https://www.ti.com/lit/ug/slau443b/slau443b.pdf)). **Priority: nice to have (footprint in rev A).**

9. **Electrode power pins (+2.5V_AE / −2.5V_AE): feed each from AVDD/AVSS after the LDOs** through a ferrite bead, then 10 Ω or a polyfuse, then 10 µF + 100 nF at the connector. Fit **DNP 0 Ω links so a passive harness carries no supply**, and add a test point on each rail. *Rationale:* buffers on the same rails as the ADS1299 cannot overdrive its inputs during power-up, which addresses TI's power-sequencing concern ([TI E2E 1653370](https://e2e.ti.com/support/data-converters-group/data-converters/f/data-converters-forum/1653370/ads1299-4-ads1299-power-sequencing)). *Budget (derived):* 17 × OPA376 ≈ 13 mA typ / 16 mA max per rail, or about 2.6 mA with OPA378. **Confirm that the −2.5 V charge pump can supply this.** The Cyton-style LM2664's rating was not checked. **Priority: must (pins and footprints) for rev A.**

10. **Impedance-excitation pin (IMP_EXC): route a GPIO through an RC low-pass and a DNP series resistor to pin 23.** Use an LEDC PWM channel; the ESP32-S3 lacks the classic ESP32's DAC, so confirm in its datasheet. *Rationale:* ADS1299 lead-off cannot see skin impedance through an active buffer. A bussed excitation line coupled into each buffer input through about 10–100 MΩ or about 1 pF is the cheapest DIY substitute for in-electrode impedance meters. For passive rev A builds, ADS1299 AC lead-off at 7.8 or 31.2 Hz is enough; it is in-band, so turn it off while recording ([TI ADS1299](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ads1299.pdf)). **Priority: must (pin + footprint) for rev A; populate later.**

11. **Guard pours and input routing.** Run the 17 input traces from connector to ADS1299 short and symmetric, with matched lengths. Surround them with a guard pour that a jumper ties to SHIELD_DRV or AGND, and stitch it with vias. Keep the charge pump, LiPo charger and ESP32-S3 antenna away from the input section. *Rationale:* the same mismatch mechanism as item 2 applies to PCB parasitics, and published ADS1299 active designs use guard rings plus ground pours and via stitching ([arXiv 2503.23338](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.23338)). **Priority: must for rev A.**

12. **USB/charging interlock: route VBUS-detect to a GPIO so firmware stops streaming and powers down BIAS when USB is present. Put the USB-C connector on the edge opposite the harness header.** *Rationale:* a USB link to mains equipment is both the safety hazard and the dominant 50 Hz path on a non-isolated board ([Autodidacts/Cerelog](https://www.autodidacts.io/cerelog-esp-eeg-affordable-openbci-like-board); [TI SBAA188](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/sbaa188/sbaa188.pdf)). **Priority: nice to have.**

Firmware notes that go with the board: turn on lead-off monitoring and open the BIAS loop when an electrode drops. TI warns that a fallen-off electrode can drive the body to a rail through the bias loop ([TI SBAA188](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/sbaa188/sbaa188.pdf)). Keep DC lead-off off during recording. Add the inter-channel-correlation check for a detached reference.

## Open gaps worth closing on the bench

Several questions the report depends on had no source. No head-to-head study compared 50 Hz amplitude across grounded-shield, driven-shield and unshielded leads at controlled electrode impedance. Close this with a one-afternoon bench test on the first harness: a resistor-and-capacitor electrode phantom, a mismatched pair, and a 50 Hz source. Typical indoor 50 Hz magnetic fields in Indian homes, and typical residual body common-mode voltage with an ADS1299 BIAS loop, were not found. The CM table above assumes about 1 mV. The −2.5 V charge pump's current capacity is unknown and has to be checked before committing to 17 OPA376 buffers. The minimum input capacitance for immunity to the ESP32-S3's 2.4 GHz radio is unknown; start at 47 pF and try 100 pF if RF artifacts appear. Numeric drift rates for gold vs Ag/AgCl, and any measurement of a mixed gold-active / Ag/AgCl-reference montage, were not accessible ([Alves 2026](https://www.tu-ilmenau.de/en/aktuelles/2026-1/new-publication-electrochemical-characterization-of-commercial-electroencephalography-bioelectrodes-in-isotonic-saline-solution) gives only qualitative results). That is why the "one metal everywhere" rule is an inference. IEC 60601-2-26 limits for EEG lead wires and the current-edition touch-proof clause were not read from the primary standard. No Indian retail source was found for DIN 42802 sockets, pogo pins, thin coax or a sub-$500 cap with FC3/FC4/CP3/CP4. Finally, the two PiEEG/Elecrow pages contradict each other on wet vs dry and on termination, so ask the seller in writing before ordering.

## Conclusion

The main finding is that the electrodes and the board's input network have to be designed together, and the Cyton inherited choices that only work for gel. A 1 nF X7R shunt is harmless at 5 kΩ and damaging at 1 MΩ. Grounded shields help at 10 kΩ and hurt at 1 MΩ. ADS1299 lead-off measures skin impedance correctly with passive electrodes and is useless with active ones. A passive-wet rev A that adds small matched input capacitors, a current-limited BIAS, a driven-shield footprint and four extra harness pins can later run dry active electrodes with no board change. The same board built to Cyton values would need a re-spin to do that.

Your measurements also reorder the priorities. The board's 0.10 µV floor is one to two orders of magnitude below what any scalp contact contributes. The four failures you saw (gold DC drift, comb hum, a floating reference, flat C3/C4 contrast) all happened at the skin. That suggests spending the budget in this order: sintered Ag/AgCl at SRB2 and BIAS first, then good contact at C3/Cz/C4, then a cap with true 10-10 positions, and amplifier refinement last. For motor imagery specifically, the C3/C4 contrast is what has to survive, so per-channel contact checks at those two sites before every run matter more than any further reduction in board noise.
